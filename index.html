Paid live streams for music venues

The room holds a few hundred. The second door holds everyone else.

Second Door Live lets venues and artists sell their shows as live streams, replays and monthly memberships, on the venue's own site, under the venue's own name. Fans pay, and after every show the venue, the band and everyone else on the bill are paid automatically, with a statement anyone involved can read.

Why venues stream, and why most don't earn from it

The room is the ceiling

A sold-out night is capped by fire code. Fans who live too far away, work late or can't get a ticket are money left at the door.

Free streams pay nobody

Putting a show on a social feed costs gear and time, and the artist and the venue see nothing back. The platform keeps the audience, too.

Splitting the take is a chore

Artist, band members, sound engineer, the bar, a promoter: working out who is owed what by hand, show after show, is where streaming plans stall.

How it works

  1. Your site, your name. Each venue group gets its own logo, colours and web address (watch.yourvenue.com). Fans see your brand, and your name is on their card statement.
  2. Book the show. Add shows by hand or pull them in from your Ticketmaster listings. The artist is invited by email and says yes to each stream, and decides whether a replay is sold.
  3. Fans buy. By card at the full price, or with membership credits at the member price. One purchase covers the live stream and the replay.
  4. Go live safely. Stream from OBS or any encoder that sends RTMP or SRT. A show only goes out once its sales cover the cost of streaming it, so a quiet night never costs the venue money.
  5. Everyone is paid. After the show, takings less card fees and the measured streaming cost are split by the show's payout plan and sent straight to each person's own bank account.

Where the money goes

Every show has one statement, the same page for the venue, the artist and each band member. It starts from what fans paid, takes off the card processor's actual fees and the streaming cost measured for that show, and splits the rest by the show's payout plan. Late replay sales are paid out the same way, months later if need be, and nothing is ever paid twice.

Payout plans are set by the venue group: a flat fee per show, an amount per sale, a percentage of gross, and shares of what's left, with guarantees ("$200 versus 60%") and caps. A touring act can bring its own deal for one night.

Memberships that fill the room as well as the stream

Each venue group sells its own monthly membership, for example $20 for 4 credits. A happy-hour set costs 1 credit and a main act 3 or 4, at any venue in the group. Members can top up with packs, and the credits they don't use aren't lost to the artists: their value goes into a monthly fund paid to that month's happy-hour acts, by how long fans watched.

Members carry a card on their phone with a code that changes every 30 seconds. Staff scan it at the bar for member happy hour, and a member who was there in person can put credits towards the show and watch it again later.
